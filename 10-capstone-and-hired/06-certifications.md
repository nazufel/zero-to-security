# Certifications

## Why this matters

Certifications are the most common question career changers ask, and the most common way they spend money they didn't need to. None are required for this course's target roles. None are free. Some genuinely help get past resume filters. The skill is knowing which one, when, and whether your portfolio already does the job.

## The honest picture

- **Many entry-level postings list a certification** as preferred, and some as required (especially government and contractor jobs). Automated resume filters sometimes screen on them.
- **A portfolio proves more than a certificate.** A cert shows you passed a test; your portfolio shows you did the work. Hiring managers who look at both usually weigh the portfolio more.
- **The best time for a cert** is when your target postings keep asking for one, and your applications aren't getting past the first screen.

## The two worth considering

> **Last verified:** 2026-10-29. Prices change; check the official pages.

| Cert | What it covers | Good for | Cost (roughly) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **[CompTIA Security+](https://www.comptia.org/certifications/security)** | Broad security fundamentals: threats, architecture, operations, governance | Help desk and SOC roles; required for many US government jobs (DoD 8140) | Several hundred US dollars |
| **[AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-cloud-practitioner/)** | AWS basics: services, shared responsibility, billing, security | Showing cloud literacy, a step toward associate-level AWS certs | About $100 |

You've covered much of the material for both in this course, especially shared responsibility, IAM, and incident response.

## Do it

1. **Check the market you're applying to.** Go back to your [Module 0 job research](../00-start-here/01-the-jobs.md), or open 10 new postings for your target role in your area. Count how many mention each certification, and whether it's "required" or "preferred." Write the counts in `career/certifications.md`.

2. **Decide with a rule:**

   - If **fewer than 3 in 10** mention a cert, don't buy one yet. Apply with your portfolio.
   - If **most** mention one, or you're targeting government or contractor work, it's probably worth it. Pick the one they name.
   - If you've applied to 20+ matching jobs with no first-round interviews, a cert may be what gets you past the filter.

   Write your decision and the reason.

3. **If you go for one, study free first.** Free resources cover these exams well:

   - [Professor Messer's Security+ course](https://www.professormesser.com/) (free videos)
   - [AWS Skill Builder](https://skillbuilder.aws/) (free Cloud Practitioner course and practice questions)

   Then map each exam objective to something in your portfolio. Where you've done it, you'll remember it far better than anything you only read.

## Break it

Price out the "collect them all" plan: Security+, Network+, A+, CySA+, an AWS associate cert, and a vendor SIEM cert. Add up the exam fees, and estimate the study hours.

Now compare: what could you add to your portfolio with the same hours? (Every Dig Deeper topic, probably.) Which would an interviewer ask more about? Certificates open some doors, but **each one past the first two usually adds less than another real project**. Write the comparison in `certifications.md`.
