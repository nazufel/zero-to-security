# The jobs you're aiming for

## Why this matters

"Get into security" is too vague to plan around. Real people get hired into specific roles with specific day-to-day work. Once you know what that work looks like, every lesson in this course has a reason to exist.

## The three roles

| Role | What a normal day looks like | What gets you hired |
|---|---|---|
| **Help desk / IT support** | Password resets, "my laptop won't connect", setting up accounts, phishing reports from coworkers | Troubleshooting out loud, clear tickets, patience with non-technical people |
| **SOC analyst** | Watching alerts, deciding which are real, digging through logs, escalating incidents | Reading logs, knowing what "normal" looks like, writing clear notes |
| **Junior cloud / security engineer** | Reviewing permissions, fixing misconfigurations, writing scripts and infrastructure code, helping during incidents | Building things in the cloud, IAM, scripting, a portfolio that proves it |

Many people start in help desk and move to SOC or engineering within a year or two. All three are real entry points.

## Do it

1. Open any free job board and search for each of these titles: `help desk`, `SOC analyst`, `junior cloud engineer`.
2. Open **three postings for each role**. Nine total.
3. Copy this table into a new file called `job-research.md` (a text editor is fine for now), and fill it in:

   ```markdown
   | Role | Company | Skills listed | Skills I recognize | Skills I don't |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   |  |  |  |  |  |
   ```

4. Count the skills that show up in more than one posting. Circle them. Those are what this course is built around.

You'll put this file in your portfolio in [lesson 3](03-git-and-github.md).

## Break it

Find a posting labeled "entry level" that asks for 5+ years of experience or five certifications. You'll find one fast.

That's a wish list, not a requirement. Hiring managers routinely interview people who meet about half of it. **Apply anyway.** The worst outcome is silence.

## With Claude

Open [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) (the free plan is fine for this module). Paste in one job posting and ask:

```text
I'm new to security. Translate this job posting into plain English.
Which skills are must-haves and which are nice-to-haves?
```

**Check the work:** compare Claude's must-have list against the skills you counted across nine postings. Claude only saw one posting. You saw the pattern. Where do you disagree, and who's right?

## Check yourself

1. A coworker reports a strange email asking for their password. Which role usually handles it first?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Help desk, usually, and then a SOC analyst if it turns out to be part of an attack. Phishing reports are one of the most common tickets in both roles.
   </details>

2. A posting says "entry level, 3 years of experience required." Should you apply?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>

   Yes. Requirements are often a wish list. A portfolio that shows you can do the work often matters more than years on paper.
   </details>
